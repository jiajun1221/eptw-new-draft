Why the System Is Being Rebuilt
Four categories of problem found in the current system
PROPOSED BEHAVIOUR
Safety gaps —the approval sequence is enforced by the screen, not the server; a rejected permit can be resubmitted into a status where nobody can approve it

===== PAGE 34 =====
Merge Mechanics — What Actually Happens
Fires automatically at Completed, never at Approved
PROPOSED BEHAVIOUR
Child reaches Completed → merge fires automatically, no manual step
Child's type letter is added to the parent's permit number
Child's full content AND its entire log history are copied into the parent, every entry tagged with the child's original permit number
The standalone child record is then DELETED —from this point it only exists inside the parent's PDF and log
A CANCELLED child (any type) never merges —it remains a standalone record permanently

===== PAGE 39 =====
Sequence Is Strictly Sequential — Not Parallel
A deliberate design decision, stated explicitly for confirmation
PROPOSED BEHAVIOUR
Individual stages act in fixed order: Host → Host Manager → Area Manager
Then group stages, in fixed order: ENV → EHS → OPS
One member's signature satisfies the whole group stage —but stages themselves never run at the same time
The sequence is enforced by the SERVER, not just the screen —closing the biggest defect in the current system

===== PAGE 40 =====
Rejection & Resubmission
One consistent rule — no route-dependent inconsistency
PROPOSED BEHAVIOUR
Rejection requires a mandatory reason
Rejecting RESTARTS the whole chain from the beginning —every reviewer signs again on the corrected version
This differs from today, where earlier approvals are sometimes kept and sometimes wiped depending on which button was used
Distinct from editing: reject is for when the REQUESTOR must supply new information; a reviewer who can fix it themselves edits instead

===== PAGE 54 =====
Amending an Approved Permit
One MT Group switch controls all post-approval editing
PROPOSED BEHAVIOUR
Content is locked to everyone EXCEPT an MT Group that is (a) selected as an approver on that specific permit, AND (b) has the edit-after-approval switch enabled
Both conditions must hold —group membership alone, without being selected on that permit, is not enough
Fields allowed to change: any permit content, including start/end dates —EXCEPT the readings table, which is never editable by anyone once saved
No reapproval is triggered by a post-approval edit —the change takes effect silently on the existing approved permit
Every edit is logged with old value, new value, who, and when —but nobody is notified automatically

? Should certain high-impact edits (e.g. extending the end date by more than X days) trigger a notification to the original approvers, even though the edit itself doesn't require reapproval?

No New Expiry Mechanism — Extension and Expiry Are Handled
by Existing Rules
